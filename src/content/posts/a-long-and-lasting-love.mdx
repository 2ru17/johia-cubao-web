> "Then Peter came to Jesus and asked, 'Lord, how many times shall I forgive my brother or sister who sins against me? Up to seven times?' Jesus answered, 'I tell you, not seven times, but seventy-seven times... Bear with each other and forgive one another if any of you has a grievance against someone. Forgive as the Lord forgave you.'" - Matthew 18:21-22, Colossians 3:13

In a broken world where relationships often fracture under the weight of disappointment and betrayal, many wonder if a love that truly lasts is even possible. Human nature instinctively keeps score: we remember offenses, nurse grievances, and withhold grace under the guise of self-protection.

When Peter proposed forgiving an offender seven times, he thought he was offering extraordinary generosity. But Jesus shattered conventional limits with His answer: seventy times seven. In the Kingdom of God, love is not a fragile emotion that evaporates when others fail; it is an enduring commitment anchored in the immeasurable debt of sin that God has already canceled on our behalf.

## The Core Message
Cultivating a long and lasting love requires daily, intentional choices:

* **The Slate is Wiped Clean:** When God forgives through the blood of Christ, He does not harbor lingering resentment or bring up our past transgressions. Receiving that immense mercy compels us to release those who have wounded us.
* **Love Keeps No Record of Wrongs:** Genuine spiritual maturity is not measured merely by years in church or theological knowledge, but by our capacity to love and forgive when it costs us our pride. Holding onto bitterness only imprisons the one holding it.
* **Love that Grows Everyday:** Lasting relationships within families and communities do not flourish accidentally. They grow through daily grace, patient listening, and the humility to ask for forgiveness when we stumble.

## Application
Is there an offense, grudge, or unresolved tension you have been holding against someone in your life? Bring it before the cross today. Choose to forgive not because they necessarily deserve it, but because you have been radically forgiven by God.

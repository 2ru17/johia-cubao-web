> "‘Lord, if it’s you,’ Peter replied, ‘tell me to come to you on the water.’ ‘Come,’ he said. Then Peter got down out of the boat, walked on the water and came toward Jesus. But when he saw the wind, he was afraid and, beginning to sink, cried out, ‘Lord, save me!’ Immediately Jesus reached out his hand and caught him." - Matthew 14:28-31

We live in a culture saturated with constant notifications, buzzing screens, algorithm-driven feeds, and endless demands. From the moment we awaken, countless worldly distractions vie for our attention. Without realizing it, we become what we binge. When our minds are constantly consumed by worries, deadlines, and noise, our spiritual vitality slowly atrophies.

When Peter stepped out of the boat into the Galilee tempest, he was doing the impossible: walking on water by the sheer word of Christ. Notice carefully: *the storm did not cause Peter to sink.* The waves were raging just as violently when his feet first touched the surface. Peter only began to sink the instant he took his eyes off Jesus and looked down at the churning waters.

## The Core Message
Walking with spiritual resilience in a distracted world requires disciplined vision:

* **The Danger of the Shift:** Crises and pressure do not have the power to derail your faith on their own. It is the subtle shift of our gaze—from the sovereignty of Christ to the magnitude of the problem—that leaves us sinking in fear and exhaustion.
* **Stop Staring at the Storm:** You cannot find peace by endlessly scrutinizing your anxieties or obsessing over temporary obstacles. Shift your gaze to the One who commands the winds and the waves.
* **Lock Eyes with Jesus:** When you keep your focus firmly locked on Christ, your footing remains secure regardless of the turbulence around you. His steady hand is always outstretched, ready to pull you upward the moment you call on His name.

## Application
Identify the primary distraction or anxious thought that has stolen your peace this week. Practice setting a firm boundary—whether by silencing your phone during morning prayer or stepping away from screen clutter—to spend uninterrupted time in God’s presence, locking your eyes solely on Him.
